Construction Printer Anatomy

The two print systems that build a house's walls: a robotic arm and a gantry, each with the mixer, pump, power and controls that feed it. Each prints any of six model homes course by course, from the same machine and the same file format. The drawings are generic. The figures are the published ones for ICON's arm, Titan, and its gantry, Vulcan.

Robotic arm printer

Fig. 1 · the newer system · 1 in layers

The mixer blends the material, the pump pushes it up the booms, and the arm lays it. The turret swings, the booms reach out and up, and the wrist holds the nozzle level. The arm stands beside the house, so the building can be taller and wider than the machine.

    Gantry printer

    Fig. 2 · the first system · 1 in layers

    One trailer mixes the material and pumps it up a tower and across the bridge. The bridge rolls along the rails, the carriage slides across it, and the mast lowers the printhead onto the wall. Everything a gantry prints has to fit inside its own frame.

      Both figures print 1 in layers so their counts compare; the arm's layer height is not published. Nozzle time is the toolpath divided by the rated bead speed, with no setup, pauses or lintels, so it is a floor, not a schedule. The arm is drawn with two booms where the published arm has four. Trailer sizes for the gantry's mixer are not published and are drawn to look right. Walls print; the slab, lintels and roof are conventional.
