Construction Printer Anatomy

The two print systems that build a house's walls: a gantry and a robotic arm, each with the mixer, pump, power and controls that feed it. Each prints a model home course by course. The drawings are generic. The figures are the published ones for ICON's gantry, Vulcan, and its arm, Titan.

Gantry printer

Fig. 1 · one-story home · 1 in layers
Tap to pause · ← → scrub

One trailer mixes the material and pumps it up a tower and across the bridge. The bridge rolls along the rails, the carriage slides across it, and the mast lowers the printhead onto the wall. Everything a gantry prints has to fit inside its own frame.

    Robotic arm printer

    Fig. 2 · two-story home · 1 in layers

    The mixer blends the material, the pump pushes it up the booms, and the arm lays it. The turret swings, the booms reach out and up, and the wrist holds the nozzle level. The arm stands beside the house, so the building can be taller and wider than the machine.

Nozzle time is the toolpath divided by the rated bead speed, with no setup, pauses or lintels, so it is a floor, not a schedule. The arm is drawn with two booms where the published arm has four. Trailer sizes for the gantry's mixer are not published and are drawn to look right. Walls print; the slab, lintels and roof are conventional.
