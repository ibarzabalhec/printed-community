Construction Printer Anatomy

The two print systems that build a house's walls: a robotic arm and a gantry, each with the mixer, pump, power and controls that feed it. Each prints any of six model homes course by course, from the same machine and the same file format. The drawings are generic. The figures are the published ones for ICON's arm, Titan, and its gantry, Vulcan.

Robotic arm printer

Fig. 1 · the newer system · 1 in layers

When the print is done, Titan folds its arm and drives off on its tracks while the conventional trades set the windows, doors, roof, paths and planting. The mixer blends the material, the pump pushes it up the booms, and the arm lays it. The turret swings, the booms reach out and up, and the wrist holds the nozzle level. The arm stands beside the house, so the building can be taller and wider than the machine.

    Gantry printer

    Fig. 2 · the first system · 1 in layers

    When the print is done, the crew takes the gantry down piece by piece while the conventional trades finish the house. One trailer mixes the material and pumps it up a tower and across the bridge. The bridge rolls along the rails, the carriage slides across it, and the mast lowers the printhead onto the wall. Everything a gantry prints has to fit inside its own frame.

      Both figures print 1 in layers so their counts compare; the arm's layer height is not published. Concrete is the toolpath times the layer height times 6.25 in of solid shell: a 12 in wall less its 4.75 to 6.75 in of foam, our arithmetic on ICON's wall section. It lands in the range of ICON's own figure, about 70 cubic yards for a 2,500 sq ft home; curved and two-story plans carry more wall per square foot. Pumping is that concrete at the machine's top rate: Titan's 3 cubic yards an hour, and for the gantry the 2019 bead (1 by 2 in at 6 in/s, about 0.9 yd³ an hour). The print clock is the estimate the page plays out: ICON's estimated 2 to 3 days of printing for a 2,500 sq ft home, taking the 3, scaled to each home's floor area; for the gantry, Reuters' three weeks of printing per Wolf Ranch home, scaled from the smallest plan, 1,574 sq ft. Both are calendar days and our arithmetic, and the conservative end of each figure. Pumping hours are the floor under that clock, not the schedule: ICON says a 2,500 sq ft home in under seven days, with an estimated 2 to 3 days of printing, and a 2,000 sq ft home in 7 to 10 days with two operators. Setup, crew shifts, steel, ties, lintels, daily inspection and weather fill the rest. The model homes print outer walls only. The arm is drawn with two booms where the published arm has four. Trailer sizes for the gantry's mixer are not published and are drawn to look right. Walls print. The slab, lintels, windows, doors, roof, paving and planting are conventional, set by the usual trades once the print is done, as the last part of each cycle shows.
